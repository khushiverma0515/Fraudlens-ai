FRAUD INVESTIGATION — COMMON INDICATORS

1. Unusual Transaction Amount

A transaction that is significantly different from a customer's normal transaction pattern may require additional review. A high transaction amount alone does not prove that a transaction is fraudulent.

2. Unusual Transaction Time

Transactions occurring at unusual times compared with a customer's normal activity can be considered a risk indicator. Time of day should be evaluated together with other transaction information.

3. Transaction Pattern Changes

A sudden change from a customer's usual transaction behavior may be relevant during fraud investigation. Examples include unusual spending patterns, unexpected transaction frequency, or transactions that differ significantly from previous activity.

4. Multiple Risk Indicators

Fraud detection should not normally depend on a single indicator. Multiple suspicious characteristics can be considered together when assessing transaction risk.

5. Fraud Detection Model Scores

A machine-learning fraud detection model can assign a probability or risk score to a transaction. A high model score indicates that the transaction resembles patterns associated with fraud in the model's training data. It does not by itself prove that fraud occurred.

6. False Positives

A legitimate transaction can sometimes be classified as suspicious. Examples may include unusually large purchases, travel-related spending, or other activity that differs from a customer's normal behavior.

7. Investigation Principle

A suspicious transaction should be investigated using multiple pieces of information rather than relying on one indicator alone. The final determination should consider the available transaction and account information.
